Background

Ticketmaster Travel helps fans book hotels alongside their event experience. While hotels were already a major part of the business, standalone hotel bookings generated 75% of total revenue. The booking experience had also grown increasingly complex, with a large number of hotel options, room configurations, and pricing combinations. I was brought in to look at the booking flow end to end and identify where we could make the experience easier to navigate while improving conversion.

Problem

The more options we offered, the harder it became for fans and travellers to make a decision. Through user surveys, Hotjar analysis, and business data, I found that users were navigating a dense selection of hotels, room options, and pricing structures without enough guidance. The challenge wasn't simply having too many choices. It was helping users understand which option was right for them without taking away their flexibility. So the question became:

How might we reduce decision-making friction while still giving fans control over their booking?

Outcome

I redesigned the hotel selection experience around two clearer paths: Recommended Bundles for fans who wanted a simpler decision, and Build Your Own for those who wanted more control. Instead of asking users to compare every possible combination upfront, the new experience surfaced curated options first while keeping customization available when needed. This created a clearer decision path without removing the flexibility that was important to the business.

Background

As the lead product designer, I created a new self-service post-booking experience for Ticketmaster Travel, helping fans and travellers manage their hotel bookings after purchase. The project started with a business need to make post-booking support more self-service and give customers more ways to manage their bookings. The experience brought key post-booking actions into one place, including booking management, required actions, room upgrades, and cancellations.

Problem

After booking, fans could also run into issues such as payment failures or problems during the booking process that required them to take action again. However, it wasn’t always clear what they needed to do next. This meant we needed to go beyond simply showing a failed status and provide clear next steps and a way to try again.

At the same time, our cancellation data showed that cancelling didn’t always mean the fan no longer needed a hotel. In some cases, the number of guests had increased or their room needs had changed, so they needed to make a new booking with different requirements. This created a need for a clear path to rebook after cancellation.

Outcome

  • Booking Management: Gave fans a central place to view their booking details and manage key post-booking actions, including cancellation and room upgrades.
  • Action Required: When a payment failed, fans could see what happened, understand what action was needed, and return to the such as cancellations flow to try again.
  • Self-service cancellation: Fans could review cancellation policies, understand refund details, and cancel their bookings without contacting Fan Support.
  • Room upgrades: Designed a post-booking room upgrade flow for the next release, extending the experience beyond the initial launch.

Impact

Manages most cancellations
BMT handled 4,454 cancellations in 2026, representing 74% of all cancellation volume.
Frees up Support time
By moving cancellation handling to BMT, up to 80% of manual Support effort can be reduced, freeing approximately 32 hours per week.
Creates retention opportunities
Changes to plans, travel dates, and hotel bookings drive most rebookings, highlighting an opportunity to retain more bookings through flexible self-service changes.

What's next?

More flexibility after booking
The next release will expand the self-service experience, allowing fans and travellers to add nights or upgrade their room directly from their booking.

Background

Loyalty Redemption was a white-label product supporting three tenants: Fanatics, AAdvantage Hotels, and AAdvantage Events. I updated the redemption experience and created a more flexible foundation that could be configured across all three tenants. Members could redeem miles in full, combine miles with cash through Split Pay, or pay by card, depending on each tenant’s rules and payment options.

The problem

The existing redemption experience wasn’t designed to support different tenants and their unique rules. Partner-specific configurations were difficult to manage, while the experience left users unsure about what they could redeem and whether their redemption had gone through.

Client reviews repeatedly surfaced the same issues across Fanatics, AAdvantage Hotels, and AAdvantage Events. Users struggled to understand how many miles they could use, received little feedback while redeeming, and couldn’t tell when they didn’t have enough miles. Some input fields also didn’t look interactive, making it unclear where they could take action.

The Solutions

Built a flexible white-label foundation that adapts to each tenant while keeping the redemption experience clear and consistent.

  • Clearer redemption: Simplified the slider and added an editable mileage input with a 1,000-mile minimum.
  • Better feedback: Added messaging and clear states throughout the redemption flow.
  • Contextual options : Showed Split Pay only when the event supported it.
  • Flexible payment: Supported full redemption, Split Pay, and card payment based on each tenant’s configuration.

What’s my design intention?

Design for understanding.

Complexity isn’t the problem. Unclear thinking is. Design should bring structure to complexity, giving people a way to understand what matters, navigate their options, and know what to do next.

What’s my design process?

Make it. Share it. Rethink it.

Making something tangible early, sharing it with the team, and letting their reactions shape what comes next. Craft isn’t just the final output. It’s a way of thinking, communicating, and moving the conversation forward.
